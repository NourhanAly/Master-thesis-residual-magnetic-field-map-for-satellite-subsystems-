Today we will be talking about our work
CubeSat Magnetic Atlas and in-Orbit Compensation of Residual Magnetic Dipole
Magnetic disturbances from satellite subsystems
 have concerned many satellite missions over the lifetime of space industry
Whether for purposes of study of surrounding field data
like in old satellites missions CASSINI and THEMIS
or for purposes of magnetic attitude control
Electromagnetic cleanness program (EMC) is a technique
is a technique that was developed to handle this problem
 through defining the magnetic sources and then either
shield them, compensate them or eliminate them to a specified limit
The identification is done through measurements in huge facilities
with external field cancellation
For a LEO cubesat that plans to use magnetic control
like PRISM cubesat
this procedure isn`t enough
 and it still suffers from residual magnetization
that disturbs its magnetic control
which requires further sources identification and elementation
Through this work
we are proposing a complementing procedure to EMC
 in which all residual magnetic disturbances are modeled
and fed to extended kalman filter to produce an atlas
that could be used in the cubesat control
to modify its performance
There are two components to consider
in magnetic attitude and control
earth's magnetic field measurement through magnetometer
and control through magnetorquers
Disturbances affect both sides
 in the first step as residual magnetic field of the satellite is measured
and  in the second step as addition of a magnetization value
The sources for this are many
and we intend to handle only wire tracks in this work
The plan is to start with satellite design
import it to our software tool
which should produce a magnetic field
and torque distribution in the satellites size
This tool would need verification and correction
which we plan to use hardware measurements for
 The final correct model should then be imported
to the satellite control simulation to test them
and establish satellite control
and maximum values allowed
in the field distribution
These values could now be used to
optimize the satellite design
Finally, the control would be implemented on the satellite

Now that the magnetic disturbance models are set
we shall proceed to describe the algorithms for mapping
identifying and rejecting the magnetic disturbances
We shall consider an example mission of
 a magnetically controlled 3U CubeSat in the ISS orbit
The attitude control is to align the body frame and the orbital frame
We will show the performance of 
a magnetometer-based Extended Kalman filter to estimate
 both the state variables and the residual magnetization
The filter maps the magnetic disturbances
 to the residual magnetic moment and the magnetometer bias
thus creating a set of maps 
for all principal regimes of the spacecraft
We use quaternion kinematics and Euler dynamical equations
to model the rotations of the spacecraft
The model includes the gravity torque 
and the control torque produced by the magnetorquers
Other environmental torques are evaluated to be
one order of magnitude smaller than the magnetic disturbance
hence they are modeled as random noise
It is important to note here the torque due to
 residual magnetization that comes directly
from the matrices described earlier
A Lyapunov-based magnetic PD controller
is used in the attitude control loop
The control loop parameters are
 5 seconds for control 
and 1 second for measurements
The plot shows the required control moment
which is of the order of ten to the power of -3
This sets a limit to the admissible level of magnetization
 If we assume a 10% error in identification
of the magnetic disturbance by EKF then
ten to the power of -3 magnetic moment distorts
the control action by 10%
which is enough to degrade the control quality
If magnetic disturbances are not identified and rejected
he spacecraft is rendered uncontrollable
 when the value of residual magnetic moment 
is already 1e-4 which is really small
